The Experience

Where curiosity meets the built environment.

Explore introduces students to construction, engineering, planning, teamwork, and project thinking through accessible, high-energy activities. Learners build, test, plan, and present while discovering the people and decisions behind buildings and infrastructure.

Explore is Aedifica's early awareness and discovery program. It is designed for younger learners and for the settings where curiosity is sparked — after-school programs, summer camps, community organizations, and school-day workshops. The goal is not to produce finished engineers. It is to open a door: to let a student see themselves, for the first time, as someone who can design, build, and improve the world around them.

Explore is also the on-ramp to Aedifica's deeper programming. Students who discover a spark here can continue into longer, more rigorous experiences such as our twelve-week middle school engineering capstone, Bridging Brilliance: Engineering the Hudson.

What You Will Gain

Discovery that builds real foundations.

Explore is short and high-energy, but the gains are genuine. Students leave with new awareness, new confidence, and new skills they carry into everything that follows.

Career awareness

Students discover that engineering and construction management are real, reachable careers — and meet the range of roles that bring a building from idea to reality.

Design-build confidence

Through making and testing, students gain confidence with design-build thinking — the idea that you can sketch something, build it, see it fail, and make it better.

Teamwork and communication

Students practice working in teams, sharing ideas, listening to feedback, and presenting their thinking to others — skills that matter in every field.

Real-world exposure

Early exposure to engineering, planning, sustainability, and community impact — so students begin to see how the built world is shaped by choices, and how those choices affect people.

Sample Activities

Hands-on from the first minute.

Explore sessions are built around making and doing. These are the kinds of activities a workshop or series draws from — mixed and matched to fit the age group, setting, and time available.

Bridge or tower design challenge

Teams design, build, and test a structure against real constraints — then improve it when it doesn't hold.

Mini site-planning activity

Students lay out a small site, balancing space, access, and competing needs the way real planners do.

Materials and load-testing demo

A hands-on demonstration of how different materials carry weight — and what happens at the breaking point.

Neighborhood infrastructure mapping

Students map the roads, bridges, water, and power around them — and start to see infrastructure they'd never noticed.

Career role-play

Students step into the roles that build a project: owner, designer, estimator, scheduler, and field manager.

Present and share

Teams present what they built and what they learned — practicing the communication every engineer needs.

Where Explore Leads

From discovery to a full engineering capstone.

When a student's curiosity catches, Explore hands off to deeper work. Our flagship middle school capstone takes the spark and turns it into a sustained, rigorous engineering experience.

Featured Program · Middle School Capstone

Bridging Brilliance: Engineering the Hudson

A twelve-week program where students work as junior engineers to design, model, build, test, and present a sustainable bridge across the Hudson River — satisfying real criteria for span, cost, structural performance, sustainability, and community impact.

Eligibility & Support

Built to fit your setting.

  • Flexible grade range. Explore can be adapted for grades 6 to 9, meeting students at the moment their interests are taking shape.

  • Many settings. After-school groups, summer camps, community-based organizations, enrichment programs, and school-day workshops all work for Explore.

  • Offered your way. Run Explore as a single workshop, a multi-session series, a summer camp module, or a partner-funded enrichment program.

  • Partnership-friendly. Explore is designed to work with community organizations and funders, including sponsorship models that keep programs free or low-cost for the families who need them most.

In Their Own Words

Ten weeks is enough to change how a student sees a bridge — and themselves.

These are reflections from the 2025 Building Bridges cohort: seventh and eighth graders who arrived describing engineering as "fixing things" and left talking about prototypes, sustainability, and what to do when a design fails. Student quotes are anonymized to protect privacy.

Featured reflection

When we mess up, this only sets you up for a new door to open.

Student reflection, post-program survey. A twelve- or thirteen-year-old naming failure as a doorway, not a dead end — the heart of the engineering design process.

Featured reflection

Engineering is designing, creating, testing, and improving solutions.

Student reflection, post-program survey. At the start, students said "designing a structure." By the end, they could describe the whole iterative process. That shift is the program working.

It is as important to learn as to succeed.

Student reflection, post-program survey. Process over outcome — a growth mindset students built by testing prototypes that didn't always hold.

Not everything will be easy.

Student reflection, post-program survey. Honest, unglamorous, and exactly what resilience sounds like at thirteen.

Engineering means designing for sustainability and using serious critical thinking skills.

Student reflection, post-program survey. Students didn't just learn to build — they learned to weigh consequences and think rigorously.

A failed test is not an ending, but useful data.

Program team observation, paraphrased from the program report. Captures the instructional design behind every build-test-revise cycle in Explore.

Families attended the final bridge presentations at Stevens Institute of Technology. As new programs run and families share their reflections, their words will appear here.
